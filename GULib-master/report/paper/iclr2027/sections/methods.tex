\subsection{Selection strategies}
\label{sec:selection}
To probe GU vulnerabilities, we construct deletion requests along two notions of node importance: how broadly a node set can propagate influence through the graph, and how strongly its removal is predicted to increase the model's target loss. We instantiate these criteria with influence maximization (IM) and influence functions (IF), respectively, using a common candidate pool $\mathcal C$ and deletion budget $k$.

\paragraph{Influence maximization (IM): propagation coverage.}
To target propagation coverage, IM seeks a set with large expected reach under the Independent Cascade model \citep{kempe2003maximizing}. Let $\operatorname{Reach}_{\omega}(S)$ denote the nodes activated from $S$, including $S$ itself, in a random cascade realization $\omega$. The objective is
\begin{equation}
\sigma(S)=\mathbb{E}_{\omega}\!\left[|\operatorname{Reach}_{\omega}(S)|\right],
\qquad
S_{\mathrm{IM}}^\star\in
\arg\max_{\substack{S\subseteq\mathcal C\\ |S|=k}}\sigma(S).
\label{eq:im-objective}
\end{equation}
Joint coverage accounts for overlap in the reach of selected nodes. We approximate this objective to obtain $S_{\mathrm{IM}}$; the estimator, optimization procedure, and parameters are given in Appendix~\ref{app:im-details}.

\paragraph{Influence functions (IF): target-loss sensitivity.}
To target loss sensitivity, IF estimates how removing a candidate would change the model's loss \citep{koh2017understanding}. At the trained parameters $\theta^\star$, let $g_{\mathcal Q}=\nabla_\theta L_{\mathcal Q}(\theta^\star)$ be the mean-loss gradient on validation nodes $\mathcal Q$, and $H$ the mean training-loss Hessian. We score candidates by
\begin{equation}
s_{\mathrm{IF}}(v;\mathcal Q)\approx
q_v^\top(H+\lambda I)^{-1}g_{\mathcal Q}.
\label{eq:if-score}
\end{equation}
where $q_v$ represents the deletion source and $\lambda$ is the damping shift. R-point uses $q_v=g_v=\nabla_\theta\ell_v(\theta^\star;\mathcal G)$, the node's own loss gradient on the fixed graph. D-full additionally includes deletion-induced changes in neighboring training losses, following graph influence formulations \citep{wu2023gif,chen2023characterizing}. This correction requires extra candidate-specific graph evaluations and gradients. For either source, we select the $k$ largest signed scores, corresponding to the largest predicted increases in target loss. Source formulas and solver details are in Appendix~\ref{app:if-details}, computational costs in Appendix~\ref{app:if-cost}, and IF variant comparisons in Appendix~\ref{app:if-comparisons}.

We quantify how differently these criteria select nodes through selected-set overlap (Appendix~\ref{app:selection-overlap}), then evaluate the resulting requests under a common GU protocol to examine how the choice of selection criterion affects GU responses.

We use Random, Degree, and PageRank as baseline selectors under the same candidate pool and deletion budget.

\subsection{Diagnosing GU vulnerability through performance decomposition}
\label{sec:decomposition}
A decline in utility after a selected deletion request can have different explanations: the removed data may also be important to a fully retrained model, or the GU procedure may produce an additional utility deficit. We distinguish these explanations using two references: full retraining on the same request and random requests at the same budget.

\paragraph{GU gap: utility deficit relative to retraining.}
Let $U_0$ denote the original model's utility, and $U_M(S)$ and $U_R(S)$ the utilities after GU and full retraining on request $S$, respectively; larger utility is better. We define the signed GU gap as $G_M(S)=U_R(S)-U_M(S)$ and decompose the observed drop as
\begin{equation}
U_0-U_M(S)
=\underbrace{U_0-U_R(S)}_{\text{change under retraining}}
+\underbrace{G_M(S)}_{\text{GU gap}}.
\label{eq:retrain-decomposition}
\end{equation}
This distinguishes requests that reduce utility even under retraining from requests under which GU falls further below that reference. The gap depends jointly on the GU method and the selected request. Positive values indicate a GU utility deficit; negative values indicate higher GU utility. Differences already present in a method's starting model are examined separately below.

\paragraph{Selector-induced gap amplification.}
To assess whether strategic selection exposes a larger deficit, compare $S_a$ with budget-matched random requests $S_r$. With $\bar G_{M,r}=\mathbb E_{S_r}[G_M(S_r)]$, define
\begin{equation}
E_M(S_a)=G_M(S_a)-\bar G_{M,r}.
\label{eq:gap-amplification}
\end{equation}
A positive $E_M$ indicates a deterioration in GU utility relative to retraining, compared with Random. This separates a large gap already observed under random requests from an additional gap associated with strategic selection. Let $\bar U_{M,r}=\mathbb E_{S_r}[U_M(S_r)]$ and $A_M(S_a)=\bar U_{M,r}-U_M(S_a)$ denote the mean random-request utility and the selector's extra utility loss on GU. Define $A_R(S_a)$ analogously for retraining. Then
\begin{equation}
\underbrace{A_M(S_a)}_{\text{selection effect on GU}}
=\underbrace{A_R(S_a)}_{\text{selection effect on retraining}}
+\underbrace{E_M(S_a)}_{\text{gap amplification}}.
\label{eq:selection-decomposition}
\end{equation}
Thus a selector can damage the retraining reference without amplifying the GU gap, or amplify the gap while having little effect on retraining.

\paragraph{Method starting point and random-request response.}
To further interpret the total drop, let $U_{M,0}$ be the method's own pre-deletion utility. We separate
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
U_0-U_M(S_a)
={}&\underbrace{U_0-U_{M,0}}_{\text{starting-point difference}}\\
&+\underbrace{U_{M,0}-\bar U_{M,r}}_{\text{random-request response}}
+\underbrace{A_M(S_a)}_{\text{selection increment}}.
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:three-part}
\end{equation}
The first term records differences present before deletion, including those of partition-based ensembles; the second measures the method's response to random deletion. Together with the selection increment, these terms distinguish changes that an aggregate drop would otherwise combine. We retain their signs and compute paired differences before aggregation. Matching rules, interpretation limits, and complementary prediction-level diagnostics are given in Appendix~\ref{app:evaluation-details}.
